With a special thanks to technology, we have information readily available at our fingertips. Yet, with today’s surplus of information also comes a vast amount of misleading information. I saw this first hand when coming across a social media post describing carbon emissions in terms of the weight of 800,000 Titanics. Certainly, there has to be a more effective way to communicate information, right? While I sure hope so, one is still bound to come across bold facts and headlines that remain unclear.

Eating Electricity

What exactly is meant by the statement a “massive data center will require as much electricity as an entire city”? Perhaps it means that the data center will use a ‘lot’ of electricity. If so, how much is a ‘lot’? Are we talking about as much as Rochester? Chicago? New York City? Or perhaps a small city that nobody outside of a fifty mile radius has ever heard of? This first question holds a lot of weight in the surrounding uncertainty. However, even if we knew the city it was being compared to, there are further questions to consider.

Weighing the Concern

Let’s imagine that the data center will use as much electricity as the entire city of Rochester… what does that mean? According to GridInfo, Rochester generates 30.2 GWh of electricity each year. Now, perhaps having a number in front of you provides clarification, but there is still more to think about. For instance, where is this number in the grand scheme of things? Is it large, small, or average? What would the implications be of a data center utilizing this much power? Would it impact the accessibility of power to residents in nearby areas? What are the costs of building the data center? Where is this data center going to be located? Will it over take residential, commercial or wilderness areas? What are we getting out of this data center? Is it providing jobs? Do the benefits outweigh the costs? Will the benefits still outweigh the costs in ten years time? The questions are endless.

Where to Go From Here

While not all of these questions are going to apply to some headline on oil usage or comparing pollution to the amount of tadpoles in a pond, they serve as a starting point to get yourself thinking. Sure, you may still not have a stance after diving deeper into what a headline means, but at the very least you may further your understanding of the information that is thrown at us each day.
